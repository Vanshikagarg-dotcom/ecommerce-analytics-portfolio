# Executive Summary

## Business performance

Analysis of 18,000 synthetic transaction records found **$2.10M** in completed-order revenue and **$1.02M** in profit, a **48.7% profit margin**. Average order value was **$126.64**.

## What matters

- **Electronics** is the largest category, generating **$607.2K** in revenue and **$269.6K** in profit.
- **North** is the leading region at **$597.5K** revenue, followed by East at **$543.1K**.
- **67%** of purchasing customers placed more than one completed order, showing a meaningful base for retention and lifecycle marketing.
- **5,305 customers** had not purchased during the most recent 90-day period. This is the priority reactivation audience; rank it by lifetime revenue before making an offer.

## Recommendations

1. Launch a holdout-tested reactivation email for high-lifetime-value, 90+ day inactive customers; measure incremental revenue rather than opens or clicks alone.
2. Protect Electronics margin by evaluating discount depth against incremental units sold, then scale the winning treatment to North and East.
3. Build lifecycle messaging around the first purchase: a 30-day cross-sell and 60-day replenishment sequence for customers without a second order.

## Limitations and next steps

The records are synthetic, so these findings demonstrate analytical method rather than real commercial performance. With production data, add returns reasons, marketing spend, shipping cost, inventory availability, and experiment exposure to enable channel ROI and causal campaign analysis.
